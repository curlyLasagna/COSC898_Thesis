In computer science courses, students are tasked with installing course dependencies while instructors struggle to support heterogeneous student's machines due to unaccounted differences. This thesis presents a multi-stage workflow powered by a custom agent skill that transforms natural language instructions, assignments, or starter codebases into reproducible Nix Flake development environments. Operating within an AI agent harness, the skill extracts requirements, synthesizes a \texttt{flake.nix} from modular templates, validates configurations through closed-loop checks, and produces a pinned \texttt{flake.lock} for single-command development shell activation. Empirical evaluations across frontier and smaller open-weight models show that the skill substantially improves synthesis reliability for the smaller model, narrowing the capability gap with frontier models without requiring an external MCP server. Clean-container and VM replays confirm 95.1\% macOS and 82.9\% Linux reproduction, complemented by an operational and supply-chain security analysis.
